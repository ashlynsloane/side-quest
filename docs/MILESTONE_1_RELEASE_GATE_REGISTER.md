# Side Quest — Milestone 1 release gate register

**Work branch:** `test/automated-athlete-acceptance-v1`
**Draft review:** [PR #12](https://github.com/ashlynsloane/side-quest/pull/12)
**Status:** DEVELOPMENT ONLY. Do not merge, ship, update the installed iPhone beta,
change a real athlete database or imply medical clearance from a test outcome.

This register distinguishes evidence generated by repeatable tests from evidence
that still requires a real athlete, provider source, or native device.

## Training-mode policy: no universal clinician approval requirement

The development branch distinguishes ordinary general training from injury
rehabilitation and return-to-sport review. An explicit **general-training**
context with no known restriction can be reviewed without demanding a
clinician-issued document. A missing medical file is not, by itself, an
injury or a reason to lock out ordinary training.

An athlete-owned append-only safety-context journal preserves protected
movements across different training plans, previous preview-specific
restriction reports, and switches back to general training. Recorded PT
restrictions, injury symptoms or protected movements take precedence over a
newly selected mode. Missing/malformed local records are not treated as
verified medical clearance. The new Profile-linked safety-context screen
records explicit training modes or additional restrictions; it cannot
retire constraints, claim clinical verification, modify exercises, or
start a workout.

The 13-gate **unapproved exercise-revision** report uses
`not_applicable` for clinician paperwork only in an explicitly routine,
unrestricted case. It continues to require missing safety information
where relevant and ALWAYS refuses executable revision confirmation because
the final write-time authorization mechanism is not implemented. This is
not evidence that the ordinary workout generator has been connected to
protected-movement matching yet. That integration and native acceptance
remain blocking tasks.

## Protected-movement gate enforcement (non-executable)

The exercise-level restriction result is now integrated into **all three**
read-only review surfaces: the draft detail, paginated inbox, and the
thirteen-gate approval prerequisites/reconciliation checklist. A candidate
with a current protected movement and a flagged exercise or missing
interpretable exercise metadata is marked **stale**, with a concrete
movement-specific hold. A shortened duration or fewer repetitions cannot
negate that hold.

The new `movement_compatibility` prerequisite distinguishes:
- **Blocked:** protected movements coexist with candidate conflicts or
  exercises needing individual review
- **Unverified:** tags detect no conflict but cannot establish clinical
  compatibility, or the training context/annotations are incomplete
- **Not applicable:** uncomplicated routine training with no recorded
  protected movement and interpretable exercise metadata

This does not require clinician paperwork for every healthy routine athlete.
The check accepts no provider clearance, returns no executable workout and
cannot approve exercise. Its catalog validation now rejects unknown
movement/avoidance tags, unrecognized load regions and all-empty movement
annotations for restricted athletes. File-backed tests cover routine,
injury-restricted, unknown-metadata and forged tag cases.

## Exercise-level restriction-tag diagnostics (non-clinical)

Added an exact-candidate, exercise-by-exercise **catalog-tag diagnostic**.
It uses preserved exercise snapshot movement patterns, avoidance tags, joint
loads, muscle-region loads, and impact levels to flag potentially conflicting
exercises against active athlete-protected movements. It does **not** claim to
infer all tissue loads, range of motion, or medical safety. High-impact and
jumping tags flag impact restrictions; loaded rotation flags spinal rotation
restrictions; shoulder-region/joint loading flags shoulder restrictions.
Potential deep hip-flexion exposure is marked **needs individual review**,
since squat/lunge tags do not establish joint angles. Missing metadata and
unqualified movement restrictions also trigger review, never a safe default.

The matcher is read-only, runs inside the existing owner-locked review snapshot,
and reports `canApplyWorkout: false`,
`matchingIsClinicalClearance: false`, and
`executableWorkoutApproved: false`. A result of "no tagged conflict
detected" means exactly that—not that an exercise is permitted. An
uncomplicated routine athlete without protected movements is not required
to obtain clinician documentation merely for ordinary training, but the
unimplemented executable-revision transaction remains a separate gate.

Synthetic tests cover high-impact, loaded rotation, shoulder loading,
potential hip flexion, unknown metadata, unrestricted routine training,
rehabilitation with no mapped restriction, empty workouts and inconsistent
routine policy. This is an annotation-based analysis, **not** a validated
rehabilitation exercise contraindication engine or native device acceptance.

## What is implemented (not executable approval)

- Read-only daily training reconciliation, readiness and feedback guidance, and bounded next-session choices.
- An append-only planning-choice journal with current in-app safety rechecks.
- Structural comparison of all reviewed exercise and workout fields; unexpected
  instructions or dose increases fail review.
- Exact-source proposal binding and durable, isolated, unapproved SQLite previews.
- Append-only **preview disposition** events: dismiss and restore-to-pending.
  A restore is not acceptance of the revised exercise prescription.
- Owner-scoped review inbox with stale, dismissed and pending states. Corrupt
  audit rows are surfaced rather than omitted.
- Critical audit-history export and fail-closed transaction rollback on deletion errors.
- Preview creation and review disposition reject impossible calendar dates
  and noncanonical UTC timestamps rather than allowing JavaScript date
  normalization to create misleading audit order. File-backed SQLite tests
  verify those invalid records leave history unchanged.
- Read-only mobile screens at `strength-review-inbox` and `strength-review-detail`,
  reachable through Profile and session adjustment. The inbox loads bounded,
  owner-verified pages of historical drafts, while the detail screen uses one
  owner-scoped service read that combines the exact dose comparison, append-only
  review history and current stale-state assessment. It displays missing or
  changed restrictions as a hold, not a clearance. Late inbox pages from a
  different athlete profile are ignored. The detail screen now has
  confirmed Dismiss/Return-to-inbox controls; their exclusive-transaction
  events affect only unapproved draft visibility, not the planning approval
  journal or workout execution.
- A planner-generated dose summary compares the exact serialized source
  to the full saved candidate. It shows unchanged units, lowered resistance,
  reduced exercise targets, and removed exercises/stations. Any altered
  candidate, modified instruction, or purported executable approval fails closed.
- Native review detail and inbox now require one **consistent, read-only SQLite
  transaction snapshot** for each complete assessment/page. Expo's ordinary
  `withTransactionAsync` can interleave unrelated work, so the native service
  uses `withExclusiveTransactionAsync` and passes its dedicated transaction
  handle to every nested repository query. The callback reader rejects writes
  and nested transactions; missing/unsupported snapshot support fails closed.
  This is a review-display safety gate, **not** executable approval.
- File-backed concurrency tests use a synthetic `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`
  transaction and attempt a competing PT-profile write from another SQLite
  connection. The synthetic writer is rejected while that transaction is held;
  a subsequent review sees the changed restriction after the transaction ends.
  Native Expo transactions use a separate transaction handle, but **native
  writer-blocking behavior has not been verified** and can differ from this
  synthetic immediate transaction. Tests also verify rollback/release of locks
  and that inbox/detail never issue unprotected queries outside the snapshot.
  Mobile SQLite acceptance mocks the native API and rejects unavailable support.
  It also holds an exclusive review read open while requesting an athlete profile
  switch, then verifies the original read finishes before activation of the
  second athlete and that the old athlete cannot be read afterward.
- The exclusive transaction API is native-only; the web review screen must
  report unavailable evidence rather than silently falling back to weaker,
  interleavable transactions. This API is not an iPhone launch test.
- Draft disposition **writes** also require the native exclusive SQLite
  transaction handle. Optimistic concurrency rejects an outdated last-seen
  event even if a dismiss-and-restore sequence returned the draft to the same
  apparent status. File-backed/mobile tests cover failed writes and rollback.
  The UI requires direct athlete confirmation for both visibility actions.
  Before the exclusive write, each event is copied to an immutable serialized
  value so a late caller mutation cannot change the persisted audit entry.
  The mobile detail screen also ignores delayed mutations after switching
  athlete profiles or opening another preview. Synthetic mobile acceptance
  verifies dismiss/restore, stale visibility remaining stale, injected SQL
  failure rollback, cold-start audit recovery and unchanged workout/ledger data.
  These are not workout confirmation, return-to-sport clearance, or release.
- The read-only detail now includes a **13-gate pre-approval diagnostic**
  that distinguishes local snapshot checks (source identity, exercise structure,
  completion, planning choices, draft status, symptoms, readiness, in-app PT
  profile) from unverified external clinician restrictions and training-source
  completeness. It separately marks executable athlete confirmation and a
  trusted write-time authorization transaction as **not implemented**.
  The app displays each gate and its current evidence limitation; even when
  all local checks pass, the returned `canConfirmExecutableWorkout` and
  `executableWorkoutApproved` values are always false. Provider and source
  claims saved with the original draft are caller assertions, **not**
  independent attestations. All checks run inside the same owner-scoped
  read-only SQLite snapshot as the historical review.
- An isolated append-only **unverified source provenance journal** now records
  athlete-reported exercise restrictions and incomplete training-history
  concerns, plus references to clinician documents/integration records as
  *unverified references only*. Entries are bound to the exact preview,
  athlete owner and source signature, checked for tampered verification
  metadata, impossible dates, conflicting claims, expiry and stale concurrent
  updates. They are copied by value before exclusive SQLite append.
- The app detail screen can explicitly flag a possible restriction or missing
  training history. These are risk-increasing, confirmed inputs only: this
  mobile API cannot assert provider clearance, reconcile outside health
  sources, or change an exercise prescription. The inbox and 13-gate
  assessment mark unresolved reported restrictions and incomplete coverage
  as blocked, even if a later unverified statement contradicts them.
- The provenance journal is included in the mobile local-data export and
  fail-closed critical-table deletion registry. File-backed SQLite and
  mobile acceptance tests cover restart recovery, source ownership,
  contradictions, stale reports, invalid metadata, failed transactions,
  changed preflight status and an unchanged workout ledger. This is a
  **provenance scaffold**, not independently authenticated provider evidence.
- The review detail now exposes a deterministic **evidence reconciliation
  checklist** derived from all thirteen approval gates. Active safety holds
  appear before missing-source reviews, followed by intentionally unavailable
  executable-approval features. Every task explains its next investigative
  step and explicitly marks `canResolveAutomatically: false`. Even a perfect
  set of local check-ins cannot clear the four permanent external/authorization
  gates. Missing/duplicate gates and forged local provider clearances fail
  checklist validation rather than hiding requirements.
- The read-only inbox verifies all provenance rows for foreign ownership or
  missing parent previews *before* applying pagination. An orphaned or
  cross-athlete safety report off the visible page fails the whole history
  instead of disappearing from the athlete's review. The detail screen shows
  a bounded unverified report history and prevents simultaneous concern and
  draft-disposition writes. Preflight and provenance expiration assessments
  use the same evaluation instant within a single database snapshot.
- A full mobile-service SQLite test creates an isolated synthetic source block,
  saves a preview, then verifies retrieval, history, read-only inbox and
  preservation of the unchanged original block and empty completion journal.

The mobile review-detail endpoint and review inbox remain read-only.
A separate, athlete-owned disposition endpoint can now append only
**dismiss** / **restore-to-inbox** events. It uses one exclusive transaction
handle, revalidates the full history and the last-seen event ID, and cannot
write an executable workout or alter the approved strength-choice journal.
Returning a stale draft to the inbox does not make it clinically reviewable.
No preview status, including `needs_review`, `pending_review` or
`reviewable_preview`, permits exercise execution or claims clinician clearance.

## Definition of the next implementation gate

A future candidate approval must validate, **inside one athlete-owned
transaction**, at least: exact source block/session, immutable candidate and
binding, fresh athlete intent, current readiness, symptoms, all relevant
provider restrictions, source coverage, no existing incompatible decision,
no completed workout, and an exercise-level dose prescription. It must fail
closed if any source is missing, expired, contradictory or untrusted.

That transaction and corresponding mobile confirmation/undo UI are **not
implemented**. In particular, do not convert a planning-minute cap to sets,
repetitions, resistance or a return-to-running dose by inference.

## Current implementation constraints

- A snapshot string comparison is not a cryptographic attestation. Local
  SQLite profile data does not prove the completeness of outside provider
  restrictions.
- Synthetic CI, JavaScript export and simulator runs never replace physical
  device acceptance or verification against the original athlete data.
- Do not silently progress training based on positive readiness/feedback.
  Changes require explicit athlete authorization; known injury restrictions
  and hard stop limits remain controlling.
